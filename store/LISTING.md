## Title
Current

## Short description (one line)
Draw copper by hand. Light the lamp. Don't let the trace burn open.

## Description
You have a power supply, a lamp, and as much copper as you can afford.

Drag your finger from the terminal to the lamp and you lay a copper trace.
Drag slowly and it comes out wide and cool. Flick and it comes out thin.
Let go, the supply energises, and you watch for two and a half seconds to
find out whether it holds.

The physics is real. Resistance is rho times length over width. Current is
what's left after the lamp takes its share. And because the same current
runs through every part of a series trace, the hottest point is always the
thinnest one — halve a width and you quadruple the heat right there.

Three ways to lose, all from the same equation. Too much resistance and the
lamp stays dark. One thin spot and the trace opens like a fuse. Too little
resistance and the filament blows. Later on the supply climbs high enough
that you have to *add* resistance on purpose without cooking your own copper.

Six sectors, six boards each, every one ending in a boss with its own rule —
three lamps in series, two branches sharing one fuse, a board that sheds heat
too slowly, a supply that rises while you watch. Plus a daily board everyone
gets, and a lab of permanent upgrades.

No tutorial. You learn it by watching copper glow.

## Controls
Drag from the **+** terminal to the lamp.
Slow drag = thick copper. Quick flick = thin copper.
Works with mouse or touch. That's the whole control scheme.
